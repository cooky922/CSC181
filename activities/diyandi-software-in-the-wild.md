## 1. User group

**Who are you designing for?**
Visitors and Iligan residents who live far (e.g. from Buruun) from the Diyandi Festival Park, known as Mugna.

**Why might this group need support during Diyandi?**
They cannot easily check the venue themselves, and they rarely get immediate news about its conditions before leaving home. They know that a wasted trip costs them time and money.

---

## 2. Situation or need

**What is this group trying to do during Diyandi?** 
They want to decide before leaving home whether Mugna's ground is in good condition, and which upcoming day would be best to visit.

---

## 3. Problem or inconvenience

**What may make this task difficult, confusing, unsafe, slow, or inconvenient?**  

Mugna can turn very muddy after heavy rain, and September is usually a rainy month, so heavy rainfall has been frequent lately. During rain itself, the decision is very easy because visitors simply can't go. The difficulty comes after the rain stops, when it's unclear whether the ground has dried enough to be comfortable. Last year, I only discovered how muddy the venue was after I arrived, which made the visit very uncomfortable. There is no single place to check the current conditions. This is an assumption based on the shared experience of visitors, including mine, at the venue after heavy rain.

---

## 4. Proposed digital solution

**What digital tool would you propose?**
A mobile-friendly application called "Mugna Ground Check" to show the current ground condition by combining the current weather with reports from visitors who are inside the venue. It also gives day-by-day outlook based on the rain forecast.

**How would it help the intended users?** 
Visitors can decide when to go, or whether to wait, before they travel or go to the venue. Each report shows when it was sent and expires after a set time, or as soon as rain falls after it.

---

## 5. Things users can do

Describe **two specific actions** that users could perform using your proposed system.

1. Check the current ground condition and the outlook for the coming days before leaving home.
2. Report the ground condition with one tap while in the venue, and confirm or update a recent report.

---

## 6. Important qualities

Identify **two qualities** that would make your proposed system useful. You may consider whether it should be easy to use, fast, reliable, safe, private, accessible, multilingual, low-data, clear, or available during high demand.

### Quality 1: Reliable

**Why does this matter to users?**  
Visitors decide whether to travel based on the app. Time-stamped reports that expire after rain keep the status trustworthy, so people don't arrive to mud after seeing an outdated "OK."

### Quality 2: Low-data and easy to use

**Why does this matter to users?**  
Visitors from outside the area may have limited mobile data or have older phones. A lightweight app with a clear status lets them check and report quickly.

---

## 7. How to tell whether the solution helped

How could you determine whether your proposed solution actually helped users?

Ask visitors after their trip whether the ground matched what the app showed; compare the predicted and reported conditions with what was actually observed at the venue; track how many visitors changed their plans because of the outlook.
